\section{System Overview}
\label{sec:system}

  \centering

\lead{Mission flow.}The controller reads the target label from \texttt{config/assessment\_mission.json} and repeats one cycle until it finds the target (\cref{fig:overview}). It selects the nearest unvisited station, plans a shortest grid path to that station's observation cell (\cref{sec:planning}), and drives the path cell by cell while the safety monitor checks every simulation step (\cref{sec:safety}). A safety event stops the move, and the controller replans from the robot's current cell. At the observation cell the robot turns to the station's observation yaw, takes four frames and classifies them (\cref{sec:perception}). If the most confident label is the target, the robot stops and holds position; otherwise it moves on to the next station.

\lead{Sensors and what they are used for.}The camera decides target identity and nothing else. GPS and the IMU, combined with the supplied occupancy grid and station coordinates, give the robot's cell and heading for planning and driving. The proximity sensors ps0--ps7 feed only the safety monitor. The controller never reads world files, texture names or simulator ground truth, and it does not use a supervisor. The supervisor-based referee and capture tools in our evaluation are separate programs that only score runs or build offline test data.

\lead{Modules and interfaces.}Each concern lives in one package, and the main loop composes them through small interfaces (\cref{tab:modules}). Motion functions return a set of safety events, empty on success, so planning and safety stay decoupled: the motion layer stops and recovers, and the main loop decides to replan.

\lead{Control loop and timing budget.}The controller runs at Webots' basic time step of 32\,ms in synchronous mode, so simulation time advances only when the controller calls \texttt{step}. Model loading and the 32\,ms inference therefore cost wall-clock time but no mission time. Mission time goes almost entirely to motion. A 0.1\,m cell takes about 25 steps (0.8\,s) at full wheel speed, an in-place $90^\circ$ turn about 0.7\,s, and the station scan about 3.5\,s per mission in total (\cref{tab:ablation}). Across the 96-mission benchmark the final controller needs 73\,s at the median and 162\,s in the worst case, which uses 68\% of the 240\,s limit (\cref{sec:evaluation}).
