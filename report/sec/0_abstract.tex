\begin{abstract}
An e-puck robot in a known Webots arena must find which of eight stations shows a requested target object, and stop at that station's observation position within four minutes without collision. The target identity is available only through a low, $160{\times}120$ camera that sees the dark lower half of each poster. We combine three parts. A DINOv3 ViT-S/16 classifier is fine-tuned on the eight reference images with view-matched augmentation; it reaches 100\% accuracy on unseen station frames at near-square poses and runs in 32\,ms on a CPU. An A* planner visits stations in greedy nearest-first order on the supplied occupancy grid. A two-layer safety monitor combines directional proximity checks with progress-based stuck detection. Scored by an independent referee, the final controller completes all three provided student missions (130--162\,s) and all 192 missions of a Monte Carlo benchmark over every start, target and station assignment (median 73\,s, worst 162\,s), with no collision and a median stopping error of 3.7\,cm. The result is insensitive to the camera scan and to start-pose perturbation. When an unmapped box blocks the route, the safety monitor raises success from 0\% to 59\%, and its remaining failures point to two concrete fixes.
\end{abstract}
